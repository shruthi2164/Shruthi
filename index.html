Understanding Donation

Why Blood Donation Matters

Blood cannot be synthetically manufactured. Patients battling cancer, surgery complications, traumatic accidents, and chronic anemia depend entirely on compassionate voluntary donors.

The Gift That Keeps Beating

A single pint (approximately 450 ml) of donated whole blood can be separated into red blood cells, plasma, and platelets — giving life-saving hope to up to three different individuals. Regular donation also stimulates your body’s marrow to produce fresh blood cells and supports cardiovascular health.

Who Can Donate

Most healthy individuals qualify for voluntary donation. Standard criteria include:

Age between 18 and 65 years old
Weight at least 50 kg (110 lbs)
Hemoglobin count: minimum 12.5 g/dL
Good general health, no active fever or flu

How Often You Can Donate

Your body naturally regenerates fluids and cells rapidly. Recommended intervals:

Whole Blood: Every 56 days (8 weeks)
Platelets: Every 7 days (up to 24 times/yr)
Plasma: Every 28 days

Frequently Asked Questions

You may feel a quick initial pinch when the sterile needle is inserted, lasting only a fraction of a second. Most donors relax comfortably during the 8-10 minute donation process with zero pain.

Your blood volume (plasma fluid) is completely replenished within 24 to 48 hours when you drink ample fluids. Your red blood cells are fully regenerated by your bone marrow within 4 to 8 weeks.

In most regions, if your tattoo or piercing was performed in a state-regulated facility using sterile single-use equipment, there is no deferral period. Otherwise, a standard 3-month waiting period applies.

Drink an extra 16 oz of water, eat a healthy meal avoiding excessive fatty foods, and ensure you are well rested. Afterward, enjoy refreshments, keep the bandage on for a few hours, and avoid strenuous heavy lifting for the rest of the day.
